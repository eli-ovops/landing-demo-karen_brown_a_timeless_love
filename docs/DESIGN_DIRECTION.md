# Design direction - Karen Brown / A Timeless Love

## Brand-asset integration pass - 2026-08-21

The supplied pink Karen Brown - A Timeless Love wordmark replaces the temporary text brand in the header and final close. The page accent is calibrated to that existing pink rather than green. Supplied award and ABIA marks form one compact recognition moment immediately after the Hero, keeping the Hero focused on Karen's role, service area and a single email CTA. Contact details remain visible and actionable at the final conversion point and in the footer.

### Footer correction

The source Logo is a screenshot rather than a production logo export. It is therefore used only in the white header, where the capture boundary can be cleanly cropped. The dark Footer uses a restrained text lockup, service area and a separated contact column, avoiding an oversized white image box at the point of brand close.

### Header-logo scale correction

The header is deliberately taller on desktop and mobile so the supplied full logo reads as a primary brand anchor, not as a small navigation icon. The Hero height is reduced by the same desktop increment to preserve the first-fold composition.

### Official-photo correction

The public A Timeless Love wedding image of Karen with Sam and Cheryl replaces the fictional Hero. It gives the opening a verified human centre. The image is used unchanged, with responsive CSS positioning only, and its official URL and local hash are recorded in the Image Ledger.

## 9.5 polish direction - 2026-08-21

The previous rebuild corrected the factual and asset problems but still used too much repeated, heavy headline treatment and a predictable left-copy/right-image cadence. This pass keeps the verified assets and business facts, but re-composes the page as a contemporary wedding editorial:

- **Direction A - Soft luxury salon:** large cream surfaces, ornate type and slow decorative motion. It would flatter the wordmark, but risks feeling like a generic wedding-template site.
- **Direction B - Documentary portrait:** Karen's real wedding image, a quiet photo-led opening and proof presented as an editorial archive. It gives the clearest answer to who the celebrant is and why couples may trust her.
- **Direction C - Contemporary invitation:** modular pink panels, energetic typography and an abstract layout. It could be memorable, but would compete with Karen's supplied identity and the real awards.

**Selected: Direction B with the lightness of C.** The page starts with Karen visibly at work, lets the official awards arrive as a compact proof moment, then moves through personal ceremony-making, experience, region and an easy contact action. Text blocks stay short and change their layout family from section to section. The page uses one pink accent, off-white/charcoal neutrals and soft 22px corners. Motion is limited to a staged Hero entrance, scroll-reveals that uncover the story in reading order, and tactile link/button feedback. Every animation uses opacity or transforms and is disabled under reduced-motion preferences.

### Client-asset correction - 2026-08-21

Visual review found three unacceptable choices: the dark system-preference theme displaced Karen's light pink identity, the portrait crop cut the groom's face, and generated stationery/landscape scenes weakened the customer-specific feeling. The current build is locked to the supplied light brand palette. It uses only the customer-origin Logo, the official Karen-with-couple photo and the two official award source files. The Hero uses the original photo's 3:2 composition so all three people remain visible. The Logo viewport now retains the full supplied lockup while hiding only the browser capture strip. No customer-visible generated image remains.

### Real-photo closure - 2026-08-21

The final visual gap was that one actual Hero photo could not carry the whole story. The page now has three distinct customer-origin wedding moments: Karen with a couple in the opening, Karen actively conducting a ceremony in the personal-approach section, and Karen with a family against the Blue Mountains in the service-area lead-in. All three are public images from the customer site and are used only once, in their native 3:2 or 4:3 ratios. No face is intentionally cropped, no generated or stock scene is rendered, and no AI enhancement changes people or event details. The final close is a light, Logo-led contact footer rather than the former dark text block.

### Editorial re-art-direction - 2026-08-21

The real-photo version still read as a competent generic landing page, not a proposal with its own point of view. The page is therefore recast as a wedding editorial rather than a sequence of equal service blocks:

- The opening remains bright and personal, with the actual couple and Karen carrying the first fold.
- Awards become one deliberate charcoal "trust archive" moment. This is a single bounded contrast event, not a system-wide dark theme, and gives the verified recognition visual weight.
- Karen conducting a ceremony is framed like a selected wedding still; the Blue Mountains family photograph creates the later people-and-place payoff.
- Headline scale is deliberately concentrated in the Hero. Subsequent headings are smaller and the repeated large-type rhythm is reduced.
- Rose offsets, fine image frames and alternating corner geometry are used as a restrained physical invitation language. Motion remains functional: the Hero enters in sequence, sections reveal once in reading order, and buttons/images respond to hover; reduced-motion disables that movement.

### Personality pass - 2026-08-21

The remaining risk is a too-long generic service narrative. The next pass removes the repeating three-part process panel and separate metric strip. Their verified content is consolidated into one photo-led answer to the actual buying question, "Why Karen?": full-time, appointed in 2014, more than 800 people married, and personally present from the first conversation through the ceremony day. This makes one business-specific proof block rather than two generic information modules, gives the actual ceremony photo a stronger role, and shortens the mobile reading path.

### Co-Pilot differentiation pass - 2026-08-21

The remaining quality gap is not polish but memorability: a competent celebrant page can still feel interchangeable. The selected additional moment therefore uses a verified, named Karen Brown service rather than a generic process, fabricated testimonial or stock-style image. The **Co-Pilot Ceremony** is presented as an interactive two-role composition: a loved one takes the storytelling role, while Karen is the experienced celebrant responsible for the professional and legal ceremony components. The interaction is intentionally small and purposeful: choosing either role changes the compact explanation and accent treatment, making the collaboration intelligible without pretending to be a booking tool. It adds a distinct visual payoff between the personal proof photo and the Blue Mountains image, remains fully usable without motion, and makes the case for Karen in a way a generic wedding template cannot.

### Type refinement - 2026-08-21

The visual voice now separates roles by type rather than adding more colour or decoration: short promise headings use the locally available Baskerville-style editorial serif, while navigation, evidence, body copy, controls and contact details retain the clear Avenir/Helvetica sans. The serif is set at a calmer weight and looser rhythm, confined to the major promise lines so the page gains ceremony and warmth without returning to repeated oversized display type or relying on an external font request.

### Spacing and close pass - 2026-08-21

The final rhythm removes empty height that was not creating a meaningful pause. The opening and recognition transition tighten, while the dark award archive keeps a clear but more compact visual weight. At the bottom, the public email and phone become a bordered action card rather than sitting loosely beside the final prompt. This produces a more intentional conversion close, gives the contact information a visual home, and preserves the mobile stack without adding another CTA or claim.

### Navigation clarity pass - 2026-08-22

The header now exposes the one named service that differentiates Karen's offer, rather than asking visitors to discover it only by scrolling. A restrained third link leads directly to the Co-Pilot Ceremony moment; a fine animated underline gives desktop navigation a clear interactive state without competing with the Logo or primary email action. The mobile header remains deliberately compact and action-led.

### PMO remediation pass - 2026-08-22

Independent PMO review identified three visual deductions that can be addressed locally: the heavy charcoal award field, the long mobile reading path and density inside the Co-Pilot panel. The award archive will therefore become a compact pale-paper proof spread with the original official marks scaled inside framed containers, so the awards read as evidence rather than a competing campaign. The mobile Co-Pilot controls and detail card reduce in height and spacing while retaining their full label and tap target. The supplied Logo and official award source files remain unchanged; only their display scale and framing are improved, because source-file provenance and production rights must not be altered or implied.

### 9.5 quick-close - 2026-08-22

The last desktop imbalance comes from forcing two award source images with very different native ratios into equal-height columns. The chosen correction makes the official winner marks and the ABIA finalist marks a single, vertically paired evidence strip in their natural horizontal proportions. This removes the undersized ABIA card and its dead space while staying shorter than the earlier dark archive. On mobile, the same two cards retain their labels but use shallow, natural ratio frames; the Co-Pilot controls remain compact rather than hiding information or reducing tap targets.

## Design read

Reading this as: a warm, trust-led wedding celebrant concept for couples planning a personal ceremony, with an editorial coastal-NSW language built in native HTML/CSS. This is a redesign-overhaul of the rejected local L0 page. `DESIGN_VARIANCE: 6`, `MOTION_INTENSITY: 2`, `VISUAL_DENSITY: 4`.

The existing concept has a valid factual core but makes its entire visual promise with one dark, wide ceremony image, excessive display serif and a repeated list-based narrative. The retained facts are Karen's celebrant role, wedding-centred core slice, service region, public contact path, full-time status, 2014 appointment and 800+ people married. The retained route and primary email CTA stay intact. The structural language is rebuilt.

## Three original directions considered

### 1. The Quiet Mountain Ceremony

- **Mood:** airy, intimate and landscape-led; more morning light than cinematic sunset.
- **Composition:** asymmetric split Hero with a calm copy panel and a full-height Blue Mountains concept image; later images move from landscape to paper-and-floral detail, then a regional closing image.
- **Personality:** attentive, articulate and prepared rather than grandiose.
- **Mobile rhythm:** image, short promise, one contact action, then alternating text/image moments with compact service cards.
- **Risk:** too much landscape could make Karen feel like a venue rather than the celebrant.

### 2. The Vow Room

- **Mood:** closer, tactile and conversational, focused on words, rings, stationery and ceremony details.
- **Composition:** still-life images anchored by a typographic editorial layout; a generous interior-like first fold and a detail gallery close.
- **Personality:** highly personal and craft-focused.
- **Mobile rhythm:** varied crop ratios create pace without dense paragraphs.
- **Risk:** it could understate the Blue Mountains and NSW coverage, which are useful verified differentiators.

### 3. The Ceremony, Held Well

- **Mood:** calm confidence with soft sunlight, mineral greens and dusty rose accents.
- **Composition:** a landscape Hero establishes the place; a mid-page letter-style image makes the ceremony feel personal; a distinct sunlit mountain close carries regional reassurance before the CTA.
- **Personality:** Karen is presented as the calm, full-time person helping couples move from their story to the ceremony day, without inventing testimonials or claiming a specific wedding.
- **Mobile rhythm:** no long text walls: short statement, visual pause, two service cards plus one supported process card, regional image, then a compact closing action.
- **Risk:** must keep concept imagery clearly internal in the ledger and avoid presenting it as Karen's portfolio.

## Selected direction: The Ceremony, Held Well

This is the strongest client-showable choice because it joins the verified service, regional context and practical reassurance into one progression: **Karen at work → personal ceremony → steady preparation → Blue Mountains reassurance → reach out**. It creates three non-repeating customer-origin visual moments, imitates no peer layout, and gives mobile a visual beat between every meaningful content block.

## Design rules for implementation

- Use a light off-white page theme with charcoal as the text and action color and muted rose as the single accent. Eucalyptus green is not treated as a brand color.
- Use a restrained heritage serif only for short display statements and a clear sans serif for all service, proof and CTA copy. Avoid oversized typography and em dashes.
- Each rendered customer-origin image is unique to one placement, used unchanged and described only as pictured on the A Timeless Love website. It is not a production-rights clearance.
- The Hero carries the only minimal `Independent concept preview` identifier. It is visually secondary to the business message.
- The Bottom 40% uses `Reassurance + Specificity + Conversion`: verified regional coverage and the preparation/on-day service scope precede one natural email CTA.
- Shown in Demo: wedding-ceremony core slice, public phone/email path, verified experience signal, regional coverage, supplied Logo and awards, and three public customer-site wedding photos. The Hero and supporting photos visibly show Karen's celebrant role without adding claims beyond their official source context.
- Reserved for Final Delivery: customer-approved photography, all service types/packages, confirmed testimonials/awards, full formal copy, final availability details, contact form and any third-party booking link.
